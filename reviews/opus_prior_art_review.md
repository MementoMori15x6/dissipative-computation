# Prior-Art and Positioning Review for "Dissipative Computation: Energetic Gating of Structure Formation and Competitive Dynamics in Discrete Systems" (D.H. Yoo)

## TL;DR
- The paper's **core mechanism — Landauer-gated per-transition execution of a Life-like rule against a local, diffusing energy budget — appears genuinely unprecedented**; no located source combines a fixed kT ln 2 per-flip price, a hard local gate that blocks the transition, and a diffusing shared reservoir on a B3/S23 rule.
- The paper's **most vulnerable finding is #5 (sticky vs. contestable occupancy)**, which recapitulates the well-established ecological distinction between lottery/replacement competition and displacement competition (Yu & Wilson 2001; Calcagno et al. 2006) and the voter/invasion-process literature (Clifford & Sudbury 1973); this must be cited and framed as *rediscovery-by-mechanism*, not as a new ecological result.
- Recommended defensible one-sentence contribution: *"We introduce Landauer-gated execution — a thermodynamically-priced, hard per-transition energy gate on Life-like cellular automata fueled by a local diffusing budget — and use it to show that structure formation requires genuine dissipative flux, that sustained energetic demand diverges precisely for rules lacking an absorbing state, and that competitive dominance under a shared field is governed by vacancy-generation capacity rather than energetic efficiency."*

## Key Findings
1. **Domain 1 (competition-colonization / spatial coexistence) is the paper's highest citation risk.** The sticky/contestable-occupancy distinction is a direct mechanistic re-encoding of the displacement-vs-lottery/replacement distinction that is central to this literature. Finding #5 is therefore best positioned as a CA-based confirmation, not a discovery.
2. **Domain 2 (voter model / invasion process) supplies the canonical minimal model of "contestable occupancy."** The voter update rule (a cell adopting a neighbor's state) is exactly contestable occupancy; the paper's K-threshold capture is a thresholded/energetically-gated generalization.
3. **Domain 3 (ecological CA and energy-based ALife) contains close cousins but no per-transition energy gate.** Coralai, Avida, and Tierra all gate survival/reproduction/CPU-time, not the execution of an individual state transition.
4. **Domain 4 (thermodynamics of CA / Landauer) confirms novelty by contrast.** Existing "thermodynamic Game of Life" work (Pomorski & Kotula) uses energy only as relabeled mass and global entropy; the reversible-CA/Landauer tradition (Toffoli, Margolus, Fredkin, Frank) prices logical irreversibility globally, never as a local per-cell budget gate.
5. **The absorbing-state criterion (demand diverges iff no quiescent state exists) connects to absorbing-state phase-transition theory** (directed percolation, Manna) but the specific framing as an energetic-demand divergence tied to Brian's Brain's lack of a quiescent configuration appears novel.

## Details

### DOMAIN 1 — Competition-colonization trade-off and spatial coexistence theory

This is the domain where the paper's finding #5 most directly overlaps existing theory, and where a referee is most likely to challenge novelty.

**Levins, R. & Culver, D. (1971). "Regional coexistence of species and competition between rare species." PNAS 68(6): 1246–1248.** The founding patch-occupancy model: an inferior competitor persists via superior colonization of empty patches. Establishes the fugitive/colonizer strategy and the competitive hierarchy assumption. *Verdict: prior art the paper must cite* as the origin of the vacancy-colonization idea underlying "sticky occupancy."

**Hastings, A. (1980). "Disturbance, coexistence, history, and competition for space." Theoretical Population Biology 18: 363–373.** Formalizes competition for space among many hierarchically ranked competitors and the role of disturbance/vacancy. *Verdict: prior art to cite* — the "attrition/disturbance frees space for colonization" logic is the ecological analog of the paper's attractor-selection-under-attrition result (finding #1).

**Tilman, D. (1994). "Competition and biodiversity in spatially structured habitats." Ecology 75(1): 2–16. DOI:10.2307/1939377.** The canonical spatial competition-colonization model: unlimited species can coexist on one resource via a competition-colonization-longevity trade-off; "species with sufficiently high dispersal rates persist in sites not occupied by superior competitors." Critically, Tilman's model assumes *displacement* — superior competitors can take over occupied sites. *Verdict: prior art the paper must cite and distinguish* — Tilman is the reference frame for both occupancy regimes.

**Nee, S. & May, R.M. (1992). "Dynamics of metapopulations: habitat destruction and competitive coexistence." J. Anim. Ecol. 61: 37–40**, and **May, R.M. & Nowak, M.A. (1994). "Superinfection, metapopulation dynamics, and the evolution of diversity." J. Theor. Biol. 170: 95–114.** The epidemiological/metapopulation analog — superinfection = a more virulent strain displacing an established one, which is precisely "contestable occupancy." *Verdict: adjacent/supporting* — May & Nowak is the epidemiological formalization of displacement competition and is a strong defensive citation for finding #5.

**Yu, D.W. & Wilson, H.B. (2001). "The competition-colonization trade-off is dead; long live the competition-colonization trade-off." American Naturalist 158(1): 49–63. DOI:10.1086/320865.** THE most important Domain-1 source. Verbatim: "the classic competition-colonization models of species coexistence assume that propagules of superior competitors can displace adults of inferior competitors (**displacement competition**). But if adults are invulnerable to displacement by propagules (as trees are to seeds), and propagules compete to replace adults that die for reasons independent of the outcome of juvenile competition (**a lottery system**), a competition-colonization trade-off alone is not able to produce coexistence." This is an exact statement of the paper's finding #5: the competitive hierarchy that holds under sticky/lottery occupancy collapses or reverses under contestable/displacement occupancy. *Verdict: prior art the paper MUST cite and distinguish — the closest conceptual overlap in the entire review.*

**Calcagno, V., Mouquet, N., Jarne, P. & David, P. (2006). "Coexistence in a metacommunity: the competition-colonization trade-off is not dead." Ecology Letters 9(8): 897–907. DOI:10.1111/j.1461-0248.2006.00930.x**, with commentary **Adler, F.R. (2006), Ecology Letters 9(8): 907–911, DOI:10.1111/j.1461-0248.2006.00943.x.** Calcagno et al. relax the strict-hierarchy and no-preemption assumptions and introduce *partial* preemption — sites "somewhere between completely available... and completely unavailable" — exactly the intermediate regime between sticky and contestable occupancy. Adler's commentary explicitly frames the spectrum from Hastings/Tilman/May-Nowak (available) to Yu & Wilson (unavailable). *Verdict: prior art to cite and distinguish* — this pair is the modern synthesis of the occupancy-availability spectrum the paper rediscovers.

**Mosquera, J. & Adler, F.R. (1998), and Kisdi, É. & Geritz, S.A.H. (2003), "replacement competition."** The term "replacement competition" (adults invulnerable, colonize only vacated sites) is the direct ecological name for "sticky occupancy." *Verdict: prior art to cite* for terminology alignment.

**Mapping (state precisely in the paper):** "Sticky occupancy" (cells change hands only upon vacancy generated by their own dynamics) = **lottery/replacement competition** (Chesson & Warner 1981; Yu & Wilson 2001; Kisdi & Geritz 2003). "Contestable occupancy" (direct capture of occupied cells) = **displacement/preemptive competition** (Tilman 1994; May & Nowak 1994). The paper's dominance hierarchy (Brian's Brain > Life > Day and Night under sticky occupancy, collapsing/reversing under contestable) recapitulates Yu & Wilson's central result that a competition-colonization hierarchy that produces coexistence under displacement fails under lottery dynamics — with the sign of the effect depending on which regime is in force.

### DOMAIN 2 — Voter model, invasion process, interacting-particle systems

**Clifford, P. & Sudbury, A. (1973). "A model for spatial conflict." Biometrika 60(3): 581–588.** The original voter model: "Two species compete for territory along their mutual boundary... the result of conflict is the invasion by one of the species of territory held by the other." A voter/invasion update — a cell adopting a neighbor's state — is the canonical minimal model of contestable occupancy. *Verdict: prior art the paper must cite* as the minimal precedent for contestable-occupancy dynamics.

**Holley, R.A. & Liggett, T.M. (1975). "Ergodic theorems for weakly interacting infinite systems and the voter model." Ann. Probab. 3(4): 643–663**, and **Liggett, T.M. (1999). Stochastic Interacting Systems: Contact, Voter and Exclusion Processes.** The rigorous foundation; distinguishes the voter model (invasion/consensus) from the contact process (which has an absorbing empty state — relevant to Domain 4's absorbing-state criterion). *Verdict: adjacent/supporting.*

**Durrett, R. & Levin, S.A. (1994). "Stochastic spatial models: a user's guide to ecological applications." Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B 343: 329–350**, and **"The importance of being discrete (and spatial)," Theor. Pop. Biol. 46: 363–394.** Establish that voter-like models with a competition-dispersal trade-off (species differing in invasion range and displacement probability) are the standard lattice formalization. *Verdict: prior art to cite* — bridges Domains 1 and 2 and shows the invasion process is where competition-colonization trade-offs are studied on lattices.

**Martinez-Garcia, R., López, C. & Vazquez, F. (2021). "Species exclusion and coexistence in a noisy voter model with a competition-colonization tradeoff." Phys. Rev. E 103, 032406; arXiv:2011.07982.** An asymmetric noisy voter model in which one species invades nearest neighbors while the other invades at any distance but establishes with probability g ≤ 1 (competitive strength), plus immigration noise; yields exclusion, mono-stable and bi-stable coexistence, and a discontinuous transition/cusp catastrophe. This is the single closest published model to the paper's Domain-1/Domain-2 intersection: it explicitly couples the competition-colonization trade-off to a voter (contestable-occupancy) update and studies competitive dominance/exclusion. *Verdict: prior art the paper must cite and distinguish* — the paper differs by (a) using deterministic Life-like rules rather than a stochastic voter rule, (b) gating capture on a K-energy threshold rather than a fixed establishment probability g, and (c) making the invasion contingent on a thermodynamic energy budget.

**Characterizing the K-threshold rule vs. the voter rule:** The voter/invasion rule is unconditional adoption of a neighbor's state with some probability; the paper's contestable-occupancy K-threshold rule makes capture contingent on an energetic threshold, so it is a *thresholded, resource-gated invasion process*. Under abundant energy it reduces toward an invasion process; under scarcity it approaches sticky occupancy. This interpolation is a genuine and defensible contribution to state clearly.

### DOMAIN 3 — Cellular automata competition, ecological CA, energy/resource ALife

**Chopard, B. & Lagrava, D. (2006). "A Cellular Automata Model for Species Competition and Evolution." ACRI 2006, LNCS 4173, pp. 277–286. Springer. DOI:10.1007/11861201_33.** Two CA rules ("genomes") compete over cellular space; fitness = cell activity (variability of state over time); low-fitness individuals copy parts of high-fitness neighbors' genomes. *Verdict: adjacent/supporting* — it pits CA rules against each other for territory (as the paper does) but uses fitness = state variability and genome-copying evolution, with no energy budget and no per-transition gate. The paper's fixed-rule competition under a shared depletable energy field is distinct.

**Galam, S., Chopard, B., Masselot, A. & Droz, M. (1998). "Competing species dynamics: qualitative advantage versus geography." Eur. Phys. J. B 4: 529–531** (and "Killer geometries in competing species dynamics," arXiv:cond-mat/0204173). Two groups war over the same territory; shows a qualitative advantage is not sufficient to win — spatial organization/geography governs the outcome. *Verdict: adjacent/supporting* — directly parallels the paper's finding that dominance is governed by expansion/vacancy-generation capacity rather than efficiency, but with no energetics.

**Dytham, C. (1995). "The effect of habitat destruction pattern on species persistence: a cellular model." Oikos 74: 340–344** (and Dytham 1994, "Habitat destruction and competitive coexistence: a cellular model," J. Anim. Ecol. 63: 490–491). A CA of competition-colonization: a competitive dominant vs. a superior disperser, with patch states destroyed/empty/occupied. *Verdict: prior art to cite* — the canonical CA implementation of the competition-colonization trade-off (Domain 1 in CA form).

**Silvertown, J. et al. (1992); Balzter, H., Braun, P. & Köhler, W. (1998), "Cellular automata models for vegetation dynamics," Ecological Modelling 107: 113–125.** Spatio-temporal Markov chain CA of multi-species plant competition (demonstrates Dytham's model as an STMC). *Verdict: adjacent/supporting* — ecological CA competition lineage.

**Axiomatic ecological-physics CA invoking Lotka's maximum energy flux principle.** Lotka's "principle of maximum energy flux" — **Lotka, A.J. (1922), "Contribution to the Energetics of Evolution," PNAS 8(6): 147–151**, verbatim: natural selection "tends to make this energy flux a maximum, so far as compatible with the constraints to which the system is subject" — holds that competitors gathering/using energy faster gain a selective advantage. This was developed by **Odum, H.T. & Pinkerton, R.C. (1955), "Time's Speed Regulator: The Optimum Efficiency for Maximum Power Output in Physical and Biological Systems," American Scientist 43(2): 331–343**, into the Maximum Power Principle — which, notably, showed that maximum power output is achieved at *intermediate* (~50%) conversion efficiency, not at maximum thermodynamic efficiency. Axiomatic ecological CA (hexagonal lattice, each cell = one microhabitat's resources for one individual, life = one iteration) implement competitive exclusion/coexistence from these energy axioms. *Verdict: adjacent/supporting, and confirms novelty by contrast* — these treat energy as a global selective principle, not as a Landauer-priced per-transition gate. The paper's finding #4 (dominance governed by vacancy-generation, NOT energetic efficiency, robust to a 200× metabolic cost increase) is a direct *counterpoint* to a naive maximum-power reading, which the paper should highlight.

**Tierra (Ray 1991) and Avida (Ofria & Wilke 2004).** Self-replicating digital organisms compete for CPU time as the explicit energy analog. Ray's own framing (per "Evolution, Ecology and Optimization of Digital Organisms"): "Organic life is viewed as utilizing energy, mostly derived from the sun, to organize matter. By analogy, digital life can be viewed as using CPU (central processing unit) time, to organize memory." In Avida (Ofria/Adami, arXiv:physics/0209081): "The heart of the avidian system is the energy metabolism of the organisms. The primary resource, without which no program can survive, is CPU time... CPU 'bonus' time is given out for those programs that have developed [computational tasks]" — accelerating execution and reproduction. *Verdict: confirms novelty by contrast* — the resource gates reproduction/execution speed and survival, NOT the execution of an individual state transition, and matter is not conserved/depletable in the relevant sense. As Tim Taylor's survey "Evolution in Virtual Worlds" (arXiv:1710.06055) states verbatim: "Organisms in systems such as Tierra and Avida compete for CPU time to execute their instructions (which the authors of these systems regard as a metaphor for competition for energy)... the matter from which they are composed can be created out of thin air (it is not conserved), and is therefore not something for which organisms compete."

**Coralai (Barbieux, A. & Canaan, R., 2024). "Coralai: Intrinsic Evolution of Embodied Neural Cellular Automata Ecosystems." ALIFE 2024 Proc., p. 114; DOI:10.1162/isal_a_00733; arXiv:2406.09654.** Neural CA organisms on an active substrate with user-defined physics; verbatim per the project description: "resource patches generate energy, diffusion spreads it, depletion removes it, weather patterns redistribute it... agents have energy budgets. They consume energy to stay active. When energy drops to zero, an agent freezes." *Verdict: prior art the paper must cite and distinguish — the best precedent for the diffusing shared depletable energy field*, but it gates organism survival/reproduction/freezing rather than per-transition execution, uses evolved NCA rather than fixed Life-like rules, and has no Landauer pricing.

**Flow-Lenia (Plantec et al. 2022, arXiv:2212.07906) and Lenia (Chan, B.W.-C., 2019, arXiv:1812.05433).** Continuous CA; Flow-Lenia enforces mass conservation and can add a resource-collection selective pressure. *Verdict: adjacent/supporting* — conserved mass and optional resource pressure, but no per-transition energy gate and no Landauer cost.

**Resource-gating distinction (state explicitly in the paper):** In Tierra/Avida the resource gates *reproduction and CPU execution rate*; in Coralai/Flow-Lenia it gates *organism survival/reproduction*; in ecological CA it gates *individual survival/birth*. In this paper alone the resource gates the *per-transition execution of the rule's own proposed bit-flip*. This is the sharpest way to state the mechanistic novelty.

### DOMAIN 4 — Thermodynamics of CA and Landauer's principle applied to CA

**Pomorski, K. & Kotula, D. (2023). "Thermodynamics in Stochastic Conway Game of Life." arXiv:2301.03195** (and follow-up "Towards quantization Conway Game of Life," arXiv:2306.15151). Energy is introduced "as the equivalent of the Game of Life mass"; temperature and entropy are computed via a global Shannon-entropy measure; the system approaches thermodynamic equilibrium and exhibits negative temperatures. *Verdict: prior art the paper must cite and distinguish* — this is the nearest-titled work ("thermodynamics" + "Game of Life"), but energy merely relabels mass and thermodynamic quantities are global; there is no local per-cell energy reservoir and no per-transition gate. This contrast is central to establishing novelty.

**Fagan (2026). "Toward a Physical Theory of Intelligence" (arXiv:2601.00021), §12.4 "Emergence of Intelligence-Like Structure in an Energy-Constrained Cellular Automaton."** Studies an energy-conserving CA (a 2D lattice of non-negative integer energy values, initialized as a concentrated asymmetric disk) and "generaliz[es] Landauer's principle to arbitrary conserved quantities," showing coherent work-producing structures arise from energetic transport, entropy gradients, and irreversible coarse-graining. *Verdict: adjacent / possibly concurrent; the single most thematically overlapping source.* It differs from the paper (energy-conserving transport dynamics, not a B3/S23 bit-flip gated by a fixed per-flip Landauer price), but because it is roughly concurrent (dated ~Jan 2026, ~6 months before this review) the author should cite it and explicitly distinguish rather than rely on it being "prior." (See Caveats — its contents were confirmed only via a targeted secondary search and should be verified by direct reading.)

**Vieira, A.P., Goles, E. & Herrmann, H.J. (2021). "Phase transitions in a conservative Game of Life." Phys. Rev. E 103, 012132; arXiv:2101.07225.** A conservative Life in which a dead-living pair swap states (conserving the number of living cells) following Conway's rules; identifies a discontinuous transition between a frozen (inactive) phase and an indefinitely active phase as the dead-cell density increases, plus a second continuous transition. *Verdict: adjacent/supporting* — a conservation-constrained Life with an inactive/frozen ("quiescent") phase; relevant to the paper's absorbing-state criterion (finding #2) and its diffusion-limited interior optimum (finding #3). The paper's mechanism (energy conservation as a per-flip budget) differs from mass/particle swapping.

**Blok, H.J. & Bergersen, B. (1997), "Synchronous versus asynchronous updating in the 'game of Life'"; Nordfalk, J. & Alstrøm, P. (1996), "Phase transitions near the Game of Life," Phys. Rev. E 54: R1025.** Stochastic extensions of Life exhibiting a Life/Death phase-transition line. *Verdict: adjacent/supporting* — establishes that Life-like rules sit near phase transitions, relevant to findings #1 and #3.

**Nevermann, D.H., Gros, C. & Lennon, J.T. (2024/2025). "A game of life with dormancy" / "Spore Life." Proc. R. Soc. B 292: 20242543; arXiv:2406.13765.** Adds a third, inactive (dormant/spore) metabolic state to Life (states A/I/D); a parameter α interpolates between GoL (α=0) and Spore Life (α=1); dormancy buffers extinction and increases active abundance. *Verdict: prior art the paper must cite and distinguish* — it is the closest "three metabolic states + Life + metabolic interpretation" precedent, but its states are metabolic labels with no energy budget and no per-transition energy cost; the resemblance to Brian's Brain's three states (Ready/Firing/Refractory) is superficial (Brian's Brain is excitable, not dormancy-based).

**Reversible CA and Landauer: Toffoli, T. & Margolus, N. (1990) "Invertible cellular automata: a review," Physica D 45: 229–253; Fredkin, E. & Toffoli, T. (1982) "Conservative logic," Int. J. Theor. Phys. 21: 219–253; Margolus, N. (1984); Frank, M.P. (2018/2019) "Physical Foundations of Landauer's Principle," arXiv:1901.10327; Bennett, C.H. (1973, 1982); Landauer, R. (1961).** This tradition prices the *logical irreversibility* of computation (kT ln 2 per bit erased) and constructs reversible CA to avoid that cost; Toffoli-type analyses note that non-invertible rules like Life require an ancillary "entropy drain." *Verdict: prior art to cite (foundational) and confirms novelty by contrast* — the Landauer cost is applied at the level of global logical irreversibility / circuit design, never as a *local per-cell energy budget gating whether a specific Life transition may execute*. Landauer's principle applied to actual CA hardware appears only in Quantum-dot Cellular Automata (QCA) circuit-dissipation work, which concerns the nanotech substrate, not Conway-style update rules.

**Absorbing-state phase transitions (finding #2 context): Hinrichsen, H. (2000) "Non-equilibrium critical phenomena and phase transitions into absorbing states," Adv. Phys. 49: 815–958; Domany, E. & Kinzel, W. (1984) "Equivalence of cellular automata to Ising models and directed percolation," PRL 53: 311–314; Manna, S.S. (1991) sandpile/Manna class.** An absorbing state is a configuration that, once entered, cannot be left; transitions from an active fluctuating phase to an absorbing phase define the directed-percolation and Manna universality classes. *Verdict: adjacent/supporting, and confirms novelty by contrast* — the CA/statistical-physics literature has a rich formal notion of absorbing/quiescent states, but the paper's specific claim (that *sustained energetic demand diverges precisely for rules lacking a quiescent "no-further-transition" configuration*, so Brian's Brain's demand diverges while Life's does not) is an *energetic* re-reading of the absorbing-state criterion and appears novel. The excitable-media literature (**Greenberg, J.M. & Hastings, S.P. 1978**; Brian's Brain, documented in Toffoli & Margolus 1987, *Cellular Automata Machines*) confirms that Brian's Brain / Greenberg-Hastings excitable rules sustain perpetual activity (traveling waves, spaceships) and lack a stable quiescent bulk — the mechanistic reason its demand diverges.

### Subagent verification on the core novelty claim

A targeted search specifically for Landauer-gated per-transition execution against a local diffusing budget found **no source combining all four defining features**: (1) a fixed per-flip price derived from Landauer's kT ln 2 (found only in QCA/reversible-circuit contexts, never for Life rules); (2) applied to a Life-like B3/S23 transition (thermodynamic-Life work uses energy only as relabeled mass + global entropy); (3) enforced as a hard local gate that blocks the transition when a per-cell reservoir is insufficient (ALife systems gate survival/reproduction/CPU speed, not the individual transition); and (4) energy supplied as per-cell income or a diffusing shared neighbor field (Coralai/Flow-Lenia have diffusing resources but gate organism survival). The novelty is in the *conjunction*.

## Assessment of the paper's five findings: novel vs. recapitulated

1. **"Structure formation requires genuine dissipative flux; attractor selection under attrition does not."** *Largely novel as stated for CA*, though it echoes far-from-equilibrium/dissipative-structures theory (Prigogine) and the ecological result that disturbance/attrition + colonization selects attractors (Hastings 1980). The energetic framing (flux required for structure, not for attractor selection) is a fresh CA contribution. **Verdict: novel, with conceptual antecedents to cite.**
2. **"Sustained energetic demand depends on whether a rule admits an absorbing/quiescent state; Brian's Brain lacks one so its demand diverges."** *The absorbing-state concept is well-established* (Hinrichsen; Domany-Kinzel; excitable media / Greenberg-Hastings), but the *energetic-demand-divergence framing* is novel. **Verdict: novel re-reading of a known structural criterion.**
3. **"A diffusion-limited interior optimum in structural complexity."** *The interior-optimum-vs-diffusion-rate claim has no located precedent*; it is distinct from Langton's edge-of-chaos (which varies a rule-table parameter, not a diffusion coefficient). It also differs from Odum & Pinkerton's intermediate-efficiency-for-maximum-power result, though that is a useful analogy to invoke. **Verdict: novel, defensible.**
4. **"Competitive dominance hierarchy (Brian's Brain > Life > Day and Night) governed by vacancy-generation/expansion capacity, not energetic efficiency, robust to 200× metabolic cost."** *The "geometry/expansion beats efficiency" result echoes Galam et al. (1998)* ("qualitative advantage vs. geography") and is a direct counterpoint to Lotka's Maximum Power Principle (Lotka 1922; Odum & Pinkerton 1955). The specific hierarchy and its robustness to metabolic cost are novel empirical CA results. **Verdict: partially novel; must cite Galam et al. and frame against maximum-power expectations.**
5. **"Hierarchy collapses/reverses when contestable occupancy replaces sticky occupancy."** *This most directly recapitulates existing ecology* — Yu & Wilson (2001) displacement-vs-lottery, Calcagno et al. (2006) preemption spectrum, May & Nowak (1994) superinfection, and the voter/invasion-process literature (Clifford & Sudbury 1973; Martinez-Garcia et al. 2021). **Verdict: recapitulates a known result via a new mechanism; cite defensively and claim only the CA/energetic instantiation, not the ecological principle.**

## Recommendations

1. **Immediately (before submission):** Add a dedicated "Relation to competition-colonization theory" paragraph citing Levins & Culver (1971), Hastings (1980), Tilman (1994), May & Nowak (1994), Yu & Wilson (2001), and Calcagno et al. (2006), and state explicitly that finding #5 is a CA/energetic instantiation of the displacement-vs-lottery distinction — not a new ecological claim. This defuses the single largest referee risk.
2. **Add a "Relation to interacting-particle systems" sentence** citing Clifford & Sudbury (1973), Durrett & Levin (1994), and especially Martinez-Garcia, López & Vazquez (2021, arXiv:2011.07982), noting your K-threshold rule is a thermodynamically-gated generalization of the invasion/voter process.
3. **In the thermodynamics section, explicitly distinguish** from Pomorski & Kotula (2023, arXiv:2301.03195) [energy = mass relabel, global entropy], the reversible-CA/Landauer tradition (Toffoli & Margolus 1990; Frank 2019) [global logical-irreversibility cost, not local gate], and cite Fagan (2026, arXiv:2601.00021) as concurrent overlapping work you distinguish on the grounds that it is energy-conserving transport rather than a per-flip Landauer gate on a Life-like rule.
4. **For findings #2 and #4,** cite Hinrichsen (2000) / Domany-Kinzel (1984) and Galam et al. (1998) respectively, and frame each as a novel energetic re-reading / counterpoint. For finding #4, explicitly contrast Lotka's Maximum Power Principle (1922; Odum & Pinkerton 1955): your result that dominance is decoupled from energetic efficiency and robust to a 200× cost increase is a strong claim precisely because it runs against the maximum-power expectation.
5. **State the one-sentence contribution** around the *conjunction* (Landauer price × hard local gate × diffusing budget × Life-like rule), since each ingredient alone has adjacent literature.
6. **Benchmark that would change the positioning:** If a referee surfaces any CA paper that gates individual transitions on a local depletable reservoir (not survival/reproduction), the novelty claim for the mechanism itself would need narrowing to the Landauer-pricing and absorbing-state-demand results. Monitor Fagan (2026) and the Coralai lineage for exactly this.

## Caveats
- **Fagan (arXiv:2601.00021)** is dated ~January 2026 and is essentially concurrent, not strictly prior; treat it as overlapping-work-to-distinguish rather than disqualifying prior art. Its contents were confirmed only via a targeted secondary search and should be verified by direct reading before citing.
- Several foundational items (Levins & Culver 1971; Nee & May 1992; Greenberg & Hastings 1978; Toffoli & Margolus 1987 *Cellular Automata Machines*; Fredkin & Toffoli 1982) were confirmed via secondary citations rather than the primary texts; page/DOI details should be verified against the originals before inclusion.
- The **Chopard & Lagrava (2006)** full text is behind a paywall; the overlap assessment rests on the abstract and citation records. The fitness definition (state variability) and genome-copying mechanism are confirmed, but the detailed two-species competition outcome was not fully accessible.
- **"Robust to 200× metabolic cost increase"** (finding #4) is the paper's own reported result; no external source corroborates or contests it, and it should be presented as an internal finding.
- The absorbing-state / directed-percolation connection (finding #2) is offered as the closest formal analog; whether Brian's Brain's demand divergence maps onto a specific universality class is not established and should not be over-claimed.
- **Odum & Pinkerton's (1955)** finding that maximum power output occurs at intermediate (~50%) efficiency is a useful conceptual bridge to your diffusion-limited interior optimum (finding #3), but the mechanisms differ (thermodynamic conversion efficiency vs. a diffusion coefficient governing energy delivery); present the parallel as analogy, not identity.
